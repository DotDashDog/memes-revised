Two days of work since the project resumed. The picture that came out is less comfortable than the plan from the summer, but it is much better evidenced. Five decisions are waiting on you; none of them blocks the work now in progress.

Waiting on you

1. What counts as "one meme"?

The clustering audit cannot be closed, and nothing can be modelled, until the unit is fixed. Three candidates: (a) a template, the reused base image, which you and the agent labels agreed on in all 56 informative pairs and which can be recovered automatically today; (b) a specific joke or trend, whatever the picture, which is what the project wants but which no method tried so far can group cleanly; (c) a broad subject (all Endgame memes), which is closer to several of your own answers and has not been measured.

My lean: not either/or. Use the template as the hard unit that can be counted now, and treat meaning as a soft property of each post (its text channel, or a written description) that the predictor reads directly, with no clustering step in between. That also answers your earlier question about what replaces the cluster-centroid vector.

Blocks: the clustering method decision and everything built on clusters.

2. What should the model predict for each meme?

Two independent simulation studies, on the two curve models the project uses, agree: for a single meme only the early growth rate and the final size can be recovered, and final size only once the peak has been seen. Peak time, peak height and the other curve parameters come back with error bars that look tight and are wrong.

Recommendation: make growth rate and final size the targets, keep only memes whose peak falls inside the observed window, and fit with a noise model that allows for bursty counts.

Blocks: curve fitting on real data and the predictor. Amends a decision accepted in May (the choice of curve summaries as targets). Neither study has been independently reviewed yet; I can run those reviews on your word.

3. Several subreddits, or r/memes alone?

The three measurements this proposal was waiting on are done. They do not argue against widening the corpus, but one of the proposal's four safeguards does not work as written: counts of distinct authors jump in mid-2023 for a reason that has nothing to do with activity (see the plot below).

Recommendation: do not accept the proposal as written. I revise that safeguard and add a control for community, then bring it back.

Blocks: the train/test split decision.

The change you approved

The image and the text are now two separate channels, and the text is embedded with a general sentence model in place of the image model's own text encoder. On your answers that moves the score from 0.68 to 0.77 (difference +0.10, range 0.00 to 0.19) at no extra cost. It is recorded as decision 0018, the code is in, and the new code reproduces the measured score exactly. One consequence to know about: the sentence model was released in September 2023, so the held-out evaluation period has to start about a year later than it would have with the image model alone.

The second attempt at the local model did not help

I rewrote its prompt to give it the subreddit and month and to ask for standard names. I expected a modest gain. There was none: every difference from the first prompt is inside the noise (for example −0.01 on your answers, range −0.07 to +0.05). The outputs show why wording is not the problem:

  • It copies the prompt's examples. The new prompt mentioned "Ice Bucket Challenge" as an example of a standard name; the model then reported an Ice Bucket Challenge reference in 7 images that have nothing to do with it. It called 14 images "Distracted Boyfriend" (another example in the prompt); one of them is.
  • It does not know what memes refer to. A July 2019 post about Naruto-running at "the raid" is the Area 51 raid to Opus and to you. The local model, told the month, wrote "Naruto" and "raid".

What this means. A small local model gives a real but modest gain over the printed text, for about a month of GPU time per ten million images. Closing the rest of the gap needs a model that knows meme culture, which means a much larger local model or a paid one. And even Opus-quality descriptions keep under half of each family together, so descriptions improve the representation without making clean clusters possible. That is the main reason for my lean on decision 1: stop requiring clusters of meaning, and let the predictor read each post's representation directly.

How the project now runs

  • One short rules file. You decide anything project-critical or costly; agents decide the rest, queue your decisions in one list, and keep working.
  • Every result carries a label for who has checked it. A result that enters the record gets a second agent that is handed the evidence, not the conclusion.
  • Sub-agents run on Opus 5.5 unless a task needs more.
  • A disk guard checks the real free space on the Windows drives before any large write, and blocks commands when a drive is nearly full.

Known and not yet fixed: the text-extraction step is slow (1 to 2 images per second); the point-process fitter has an error in one likelihood term and often stops short of the best fit. Neither affects a result quoted above.
