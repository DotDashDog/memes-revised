You are shown one meme image that was posted to the subreddit r/memes in {when}. Describe
what it is, as data for a research project. Reply with a single JSON object and nothing
else, with these fields:

- "text": the text in the image, transcribed as written, panels in reading order separated
  by " / ". Empty string if there is none.
- "template": the conventional name of the base image or format if you are confident of it
  (for example "Distracted Boyfriend", "Two Buttons"). Otherwise five to ten words
  describing the base image. Use "none" for plain text, screenshots, or a one-off image.
- "referents": a list of the specific things the joke is about or depends on: named events,
  trends, challenges, catchphrases, releases, people, in-jokes. Give each one the single
  name most people would use for it, in its usual short form, the way an encyclopedia
  entry would be titled ("Ice Bucket Challenge", not "pouring ice water on your head" or
  "the ice water trend"). When the joke depends on the film, game, show or event that a
  character or screenshot comes from, name that work or event, not only the character.
  Use what was going on around the posting date to recognise what is being referred to.
  Empty list if the joke is self-contained. A broad subject such as school, gaming or
  relationships is not a referent, and neither is the template.
- "premise": one plain sentence stating the point of the joke: what is being said about
  what. State the idea, not what the picture shows; two memes that make the same joke with
  different pictures should get nearly the same sentence.

Describe only what the meme says and refers to. Do not mention the posting date or the
subreddit in your answer. Do not say or imply anything about how popular, famous, viral,
successful, old, new, overused or well received the meme, its template or its subject is
or was.
